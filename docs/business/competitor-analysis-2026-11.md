# Competitor analysis and the ten changes most likely to make money

17 November 2026. The working notes, in the order things were found, are in `competitor-analysis-log-2026-11.md`.

## The short version

TapWater.uk has more data than either direct rival and is the only site in the field with a softener quote route. It is behind on charging for anything. MyTapWater, a one-person company with about 105 district pages, sells reports at £19.99 and £39.99 and licenses data to a Culligan-backed filter brand. We have 2,152 districts with hardness readings and currently earn about £100 a month.

None of the five competitors does what the installer marketplace does. MyTapWater says it "does not sell water softeners", TapWaterData has no UK softener route at all, Our Taap sells its own machine, Harvey sells only its own brand through a phone-required home demo, and MyJobQuote sends softener jobs to general plumbers with no hardness figure attached.

Aqua Cure, UK Water Filters and HaMuch were looked at and left out: the first two repeat Harvey's pattern at smaller scale, and HaMuch blocked every page read.

### What each one teaches

- **MyTapWater** puts a paid product directly under the free answer and publishes business prices. It is thin everywhere else: no named people, dormant accounts, hardness figures that disagree with each other, no softener route.
- **TapWaterData** shows which topics pull traffic we do not have (hosepipe bans by company, storm overflows by postcode) and that a small email list can be sold as a sponsor slot. In the UK it earns almost nothing: three products, all linking to the US Amazon store.
- **Harvey** hides its prices, has no local pages and asks for a phone number and an hour at home. It already accepts referred customers from other businesses through co-branded pages.
- **MyJobQuote** sets the price reference for leads and shows what tradespeople hate: recycled leads, a 48-hour dispute window, credits that expire.
- **Our Taap** proves a filter brand will pay for postcode data, and pays affiliates a fixed £60 per two-year subscription.

## The ten changes, ranked

Ranked by money over the next six months, weighted by how good the evidence is. Monthly figures are my estimates, with the sum shown so you can change the assumptions.

### 1. Sign the first two or three installers, starting with the Harvey TwinTec dealers

**Evidence**
- 21 quote requests in the last 28 days (19 people), none sold. Requests ran at 13 a month in September and October.
- No competitor serves this visitor: the three data sites have no softener route, and Harvey books installs only in "core coverage areas (e.g., London and Home Counties)" for its own brand.
- Independent dealers already pay to be found: Checkatrade charges £30 or from £59 a month on 12-month terms.
- £35 a lead shared by at most two sits inside the observed range: MyBuilder "£2 to £35", one MyJobQuote review implying about £39 for a lead shared by three, Bark reported at £14 to £22 plus VAT shared by five. It is 2.8% of MyJobQuote's £1,250 average softener job.
- Three dealers named in the Harvey research (UK Water Softeners, Thames Valley Softeners, West London Water Softeners) are already on the outreach list written today.

**Estimate:** 19 people a month, half in an area with an installer, sold to one or two at £35: about £330 to £665 a month now, rising with lead volume.

**First step:** the calls and emails in `installer-outreach-2026-11.md`. Add to `/partners`: leads never expire, whether £35 includes VAT, and that every lead carries the measured hardness. Those three points answer the commonest complaints about MyJobQuote and Bark.

### 2. Answer the two business enquiries and put the data product in front of filter brands

**Evidence**
- LPRS Connected Solutions asked on 29 September for "water hardness information for use with water filters", 1,000 to 10,000 lookups a month. A second enquiry came on 6 November. Both are still marked new; their alerts went to the dead mailbox.
- MyTapWater charges £199 a month for 5,000 lookups and £599 for 25,000, and names Our Taap as a customer. Our Taap's checker confirms it: "Water quality data supplied by MyTapWater."
- Our prices are £99 for 5,000 and £249 for 50,000, with a free 90-day pilot. MyTapWater has no free tier.
- Waterdrop, Doulton and the softener dealers have no postcode checker of their own.

**Estimate:** one customer at our Starter or Growth price is £99 to £249 a month. Matching MyTapWater's prices would double that.

**First step:** reply to LPRS this week with a pilot key. Then decide whether to raise Starter to £199; the only competitor with a paying customer charges that.

### 3. Build town pages for hardness and for softener installation

**Evidence**
- Hardness searches showed us 38,678 times in 90 days; 21,524 of those were below position 10. When they land on a postcode page the average position is 43.
- Where a `/hardness/[area]` page exists it ranks between 3 and 8.
- Harvey has no town pages and did not appear for "water softener Reading". The local results are held by HaMuch and thin cost-guide farms, none of which shows a hardness figure.
- Postcode pages turn search visits into requests at about 1 in 43.

**Estimate:** lifting click-through on the 7,200 monthly below-the-fold impressions from about 1% to 4% adds roughly 220 visits and, at the postcode rate, 5 requests a month: £175 to £350. The larger gain is searches we do not appear for at all, which I cannot size.

**First step:** we only hold local-authority names (278, of which 148 are hard). Add a post-town list, then generate "Water hardness in {town}" with the supplier, zone, reading and date, and a quote form.

### 4. Publish softener prices where Harvey hides them

**Evidence**
- Harvey's "cost" article has no pound figures. Its only public numbers are "from £1,195" on a footer page and a £2,300 finance example at 11.9% APR over three or five years.
- Softener searches showed us 10,487 times in 90 days at an average position of 32.7, for 15 clicks.
- The cost search is won by MyJobQuote's single guide ("The average cost to install a water softener is £1,250"), not by Harvey.

**Estimate:** no separate figure; this feeds requests into changes 1 and 3.

**First step:** rebuild `/guides/water-softener-cost-uk` around a price table by brand with sources and dates, including Harvey's own figures, and show the local price range beside every quote form.

### 5. Test a paid report under the free result

**Evidence**
- MyTapWater places "Unlock your full water report … £19.99" immediately after the score, with the postcode carried into a hosted checkout, and sells a £39.99 property version to buyers and landlords.
- It did not appear in a search for house-purchase water reports, so that position is open.
- Against: there is no sign of how many it sells. No reviews, no testimonials, and the company has only filed dormant accounts.

**Estimate:** 856 search visits to postcode pages in 28 days, plus visitors who arrive through the homepage search, so call it 1,200 a month; at 0.5% buying a £19.99 report, roughly £120 a month. The conversion rate is a guess.

**First step:** put a "Full report for {postcode}, £19.99" button on postcode pages that leads to a short waiting-list form, and count clicks for two weeks before building the PDF or taking payment.

### 6. Add Our Taap and move the best-paying products into the busy slots

**Evidence**
- Our Taap pays £60 for a 24-month plan, £30 for 12 months, on a 30-day cookie (Awin 122454, read today). You are already on Awin.
- Postcode pages produced 458 affiliate clicks in 28 days. 206 went to the Waterdrop 10UA, a roughly £45 filter paying about £4.50 a sale at 10%.
- 108 went to the ZeroWater jug through Amazon at 3%. ZeroWater UK was reported at 8% on a network; I did not verify that.
- Soft-water districts (roughly 357 of 856 postcode search clicks) have no softener route, so products are their only money path.

**Estimate:** two or three Our Taap subscriptions a month would be £120 to £180, about what all current affiliate links earn together. Applicants must pass a credit check, so some will be declined.

**First step:** apply on Awin. Offer it on taste and chlorine results, with an honest comparison of £35.99 a month for 24 months against a one-off under-sink filter.

### 7. Ask Harvey for a referral page, for areas where no dealer signs up

**Evidence**
- Harvey runs co-branded referral pages with a promo code for Schmidt, Mockford & Hunt and Wright Clean.
- North and north-west London has three open requests and no independent dealer was found there.
- Against: what Harvey pays the referrer was not found, and Harvey sells only its own brand.

**Estimate:** not possible until Harvey states a figure.

**First step:** one email asking for referral terms. Use it only where no installer covers the district, so it never competes with paying dealers.

### 8. Turn the water-testing guide into a place people can order a test

**Evidence**
- Testing searches showed us 17,999 times in 90 days at an average position of 31.1, for 35 clicks.
- "How to test water quality" showed 1,719 times at position 4.9 and got no clicks. "Water testing near me" sits at 10.5.
- TapWaterData says "We haven't verified a lead test kit for UK buyers yet." MyTapWater sells no test.
- Against: I found no UK lab with an affiliate programme. Lab tests retail at about £140 to £600, strip kits at £30 to £40.

**Estimate:** not possible without a partner.

**First step:** rewrite the title and opening of the testing guide for the position-4.9 search, list the free test each water company offers, and approach one UK lab for a referral fee.

### 9. Move the alert signup up the page and promise something specific

**Evidence**
- We gain about 7 subscribers a month from roughly 5,000 visits. The form is the last block on the postcode page, and the five-email series ends after 30 days. No affiliate click in the last 28 days came from email.
- MyTapWater promises alerts for "supply incidents, hosepipe bans"; TapWaterData runs a separate "Get UK hosepipe news" list on every postcode page.
- TapWaterData sells one sponsor slot per issue at $300 against 4,727 subscribers.
- We already check incidents every 15 minutes, which neither rival's static pages can match.

**Estimate:** nothing in the first months. At 2,000 subscribers a sponsor slot priced like TapWaterData's would be about £200 an issue.

**First step:** place "Email me if there is an incident or hosepipe ban in {district}" directly under the verdict on the postcode page.

### 10. Add hosepipe-ban status and storm overflows to every postcode page

**Evidence**
- TapWaterData has 20 "Is there a {company} hosepipe ban right now?" pages. Its status was last checked on 11 October, 37 days before the read, with five large companies blank.
- MyTapWater's lead-pipe checker was the first result in the one search run for it.
- We showed for these topics 496 times in 90 days.
- Soft-water areas currently have little to say; overflow counts give every district a local fact.

**Estimate:** traffic and subscribers, not direct money. Hosepipe searches are seasonal, so this pays from spring.

**First step:** a daily check of each company's restrictions page feeding one status line per district, built before April.

## What I could not establish

- UK Google rankings for any competitor. The search tool is US-based, so "appeared" and "absent" are weak signals.
- Whether MyTapWater's reports sell, what Our Taap pays for its data, and what Harvey pays referrers.
- HaMuch's pages, page count and prices (blocked); Bark's lead prices come from a third party.
- Our own income. The £100 a month is still my estimate; the Amazon, Awin and Osmio dashboards have not been reconciled.
- Whether town pages would convert at the postcode-page rate.
